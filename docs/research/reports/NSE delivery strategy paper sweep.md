# Pre-Register Promoter Open-Market Buying Next

**Bottom line.** No strategy in the eleven-note sweep is demonstrated net of a 35-40 bp round trip on Indian data: every Indian number is gross, most come from samples that end in 2014 or earlier, and none has a true out-of-sample record. The strongest candidate to pre-register next is **promoter open-market buying, traded long-only at the next open after the exchange broadcast**, because it is the only Indian mean that clears the cost bar on paper (a five-day abnormal return of **+182 bp mean, +59 bp median, N = 3,027**, 2006-2014), it is a genuinely new return source rather than a re-cut of price momentum, and it faces the lowest family bar in the project's protocol (**t >= 2.24**, insider-buying family N = 2). Its case is fragile: the median is only 1.5 to 1.8 times the cost bar, the paper's window starts on the disclosure day while a retail buyer can act no earlier than about trade day + 3 to 4, and the one other Indian study found long-only insider buys earned **-20 to -90 bp** over 1-12 months. The binding constraint is data, not cost: the test needs point-in-time insider filings, and **NSE's Terms of Use prohibit "systematic or automated data collection"**, so the data has to come from written permission, a licensed NSE product, or a manual export by the owner. The next best items are measurements on the existing book rather than new strategies: a scaled-turnover split of the trial-126 trade list, a rank-band cost overlay, and a low-volatility gate judged on non-inferiority. Everything that needs point-in-time fundamentals is blocked, and a long list of calendar, reversal, flow and machine-learning ideas is dead and should not be re-run.

## The evidence is entirely gross, old and thin

The eleven notes (00 to 10) label each fact as a cited finding, an inference or a gap, which this report maps to the brief's [C], [I] and [G]. Files 08 and 10 add a second layer: [P] means the author read the primary source, [S] means a secondary source, snippet or abstract only, and [C] there means the author's own arithmetic, which collides with the brief's [C]; this report therefore writes "author arithmetic" in words and carries every "abstract-only", "snippet" and "not verified" flag through unchanged. Nothing below upgrades an inference, a blog backtest or a press anecdote into a verified finding.

The yardstick is the project's cost model: a delivery round trip of **33.05 bp at Rs 33k per position and 39.05 bp with doubled slippage**, rising to about 41 bp at Rs 3 lakh, 56-60 bp at Rs 5-6.7k tickets, and 493 bp at Rs 367 because of the Rs 15.34 depository charge per sell scrip, with STT at 0.1% a side and short-term gains taxed at 20% (file 00, section 6). The exclusion map in file 00 closes all intraday work, overnight ETF, post-earnings drift, short-term reversal, the Sahab Ranker, time-series foundation models, 52-week-high and residual momentum, lookback grids, breadth and intermarket signals, bulk and block deals, and the NSE filing event study, and it records a count of record of 204 trials with the momentum family at N = 11 (next bar **t >= 2.865**). The live book (trial 126: monthly, top 15, equal weight, 200 most-traded names) nets **+0.71% a month at t 2.51**, and its after-tax replay roughly ties the Nifty200 Momentum 30 index fund (17.67% against 17.28%). A new idea therefore has to be a different return source, or a cost or risk lever on that book, not another price-momentum variant.

Two cautions shape every verdict. First, the published Indian evidence decays: the HDFC deck shows the Momentum 30 index's excess over the Nifty 200 shrinking from +4.3 points a year over ten years to **+0.8 over five**, and the backtest-to-live CAGR gap narrowing from 5.6 to 2.3 points ([HDFC deck, July 2026](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2026-07/HDFC%20NIFTY200%20Momentum%2030%20Index%20Fund_PPT%20%28July%202026%29.pdf)). Second, no one has published a net-of-cost, out-of-sample Indian delivery result that survives a multiple-testing bar; the closest are practitioner blogs with post-hoc splits and no t-statistics. Vendor records (Tradetron, Streak, smallcase) are unverifiable and were discarded.

The table's central message is that cost is rarely the obstacle for slow delivery strategies; the obstacles are event counts, unreplicated sample periods and data access. The Joshipura row is the only slow-reversal variant with Indian evidence and a trivial cost, but it comes from a 1995-2008 sample and, given three-year formation, offers about four independent windows since 2013, so it cannot reach any family bar in a reasonable time ([Joshipura, NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/res_paperfinal223.pdf)). The fundamentals row shows the decay problem in numbers: the same factors that look decisive over the full JKP sample (t up to 4.3) fall to t 1.7-2.1 in 2015-2025 ([JKP factors](https://jkpfactors.com)). The Freefincal conditioner is the most interesting zero-cost item, but its two studies disagree by a factor of three on the size of the momentum effect (10.92 versus 3.15 points), which is itself evidence that the result depends on construction ([Freefincal study 1](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/); [Freefincal study 2](https://freefincal.com/does-factor-investing-outperformance-fail-for-high-turnover-stocks/)). On exclusion, an abstract-only claim ([Chhatwani](https://publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/price-and-volume-effects-of-nifty-50-index-reorganization)) is contradicted by a primary paper whose clean deletions show a permanent negative drift and no rebound ([Parthasarathy 2019](https://macrothink.org/journal/index.php/ijafr/article/view/15320)), so the verdict is dead, not "promising".

## Why promoter open-market buying goes first, and how it could fail

Promoter buying is the right next pre-registration for four reasons. It is the only candidate whose Indian mean sits several multiples above the cost bar. It is already sanctioned in the project's own planning as "Trial 2" (file 00, row 7 of the 3e list, and the Pivot B brief), so it needs no new mandate. It draws on a different information source (a person who knows the firm paying their own money) than any price-based family the project has closed. And because it is a new family at N = 2, it faces t >= 2.24 rather than the momentum family's 2.865. The primary Indian evidence is the Brochet-Lee-Srinivasan paper, which covers 2006-2014 and finds that open-market purchases reported before earnings precede abnormal returns, with the effect concentrated in promoters ([Brochet, Lee and Srinivasan 2017](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Brochet%20Lee%20Srinivasan%202017%20-%20The%20Role%20of%20Insider%20Trading%20in%20the%20Market%20Reaction%20to%20Earnings%20News.pdf)). Its 3,027 events work out to roughly 336 a year across all of NSE (author arithmetic), which is enough events for a real test if the universe is not cut down too far.

Four things could sink it, and a pre-registration should name them in advance. First, the entry lag: the paper's five-day window starts on the disclosure day itself, while in a one-week sample from February 2016 the median trade-to-broadcast lag was 2 weekdays and broadcasts often landed after the 15:30 close, so the earliest retail entry is about T+3 to T+4 and part of the pop is gone; no paper measures the return from the next open after broadcast in India, and that number decides the strategy. Second, the other Indian study, which starts at trade day +2, found long-only insider buys negative over 1-12 months and the signal living on the sell side (-7.32% at 12 months), which a delivery account cannot short ([Krishnan and Rangan, NSE-IGIDR](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/research/content/NSE-IGIDR-WP4-14-15.pdf)). Third, regime change: both papers predate the new insider-trading disclosure regime, which the notes date to May 2015 without an independent source, and no newer Indian study was found. Fourth, universe: promoter buying likely skews toward smaller names than the project's 200 most-traded universe, where the project lacks adequate history beyond rank 400; how many events fall inside the tradable universe is unknown and is my inference, not a sourced fact.

A defensible pre-registration would fix, before any return is computed, one primary horizon (five trading days from the next open after broadcast, matching the paper), a market-adjusted benchmark, an event definition (open-market purchases by promoters, with any value threshold set from the filing data alone and not from returns), clustering by date for the t-statistic, both halves of the sample passing at t >= 2.24 net of 33-39 bp, and 2026 held out. Longer horizons would be reported as descriptive only, since each extra horizon adds trials. If the net median turns out to sit near zero, the honest result is a closed negative, which is still worth the small cost. My own estimate is that the chance of a net-positive, both-halves pass is below even, mostly because of the entry lag and the age of the evidence; that is a judgement, not a sourced figure.

The fallback ordering if the data is not obtainable is: the scaled-turnover split on the trial-126 trade list (no new data), then rank bands and the low-volatility gate as non-inferiority checks, then the three-year contrarian as its own small family.

## NSE's terms forbid the data collection this test needs

The insider-trading feed is technically reachable: one request for 1-7 February 2016 to the exchange's corporate-filings endpoint returned HTTP 200 with 418 rows and 32 fields, including trade dates, intimation date and the exchange broadcast timestamp, which is exactly what the entry-lag test needs. Contractually it is not permitted. The NSE Terms of Use, page marked "Updated on: 29/10/2025", state: "User is prohibited to conduct any systematic or automated data collection activities (including scraping, data mining, data extraction and data harvesting) on or in relation to our Website / Mobile Application", and separately bar content from being "copied, modified, reverse engineer, reproduced, uploaded, transmitted, posted, stored (either in hardcopy or in an electronic retrieval system) ... without prior written permission" ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/static/nse-terms-of-use)). The 96-byte robots.txt (`User-agent: * Allow: / Disallow: /market-data-test`) permits crawling of most paths but does not override the Terms of Use ([NSE robots.txt](https://www.nseindia.com/robots.txt)). Other sites' robots files restrict bots too, and robots is not a licence; the niftyindices.com terms were not read at all.

The routes that respect the constraint are written permission from NSE, a licensed NSE Data and Analytics product (whether one sells a PIT history, and its price, was not checked), or the owner exporting date ranges by hand from the [NSE insider-trading page](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-insider-trading). Third-party archives such as Trendlyne show insider tables, but their export format, plan limits and licence were not established. Two practical warnings follow from the sweep itself. The earlier research probed the exchange API with a browser User-Agent and a cookie session, and a follow-up loop of yearly "Offer for Sale" calls was denied by the permission classifier as "Bot Check Circumvention"; that approach should neither be relied on nor repeated, and the February 2016 probe is evidence of feasibility only, not a licence to pull a decade of history. Separately, the same clause also reads on storage, so the owner may want to check how the NSE filing events and other NSE-sourced files already held (9,586 filing events, bhavcopy) were obtained; the sweep did not audit that.

## Dead ends: do not re-run these

These were closed by file 00, by the sweep's own reading, or both. Short-term and weekly reversal (trials 121-122, and a long-short GitHub repo whose smoothed-reversal net Sharpe of 0.53 at 10 bp turns to -0.51 at 20 bp, [abhinav77071/nse-reversal-momentum](https://github.com/abhinav77071/nse-reversal-momentum)). Overnight and intraday effects. The 52-week high and residual momentum. Moving-average and chart-pattern rules. Post-earnings drift and standardised-surprise signals. Pairs and statistical arbitrage. The single-stock-futures basis and ETF premium trades. Lead-lag signals. FII and DII flow rules. The delivery-percentage rule, rejected by decision rather than by a measured look. Bulk and block deals (the L50 look is void). Surveillance and circuit lists such as ASM, GSM and price bands, usable only as avoid flags. Machine-learning and LLM stock selection on Indian data. Quality, profitability and low-leverage factors as return sources. Do-it-yourself Nifty 50 rotations, which in the most careful open-source study trail a Momentum 30 index fund by 4.5 to 11.1 points a year ([niftyshop-backtester](https://github.com/vishalvx/niftyshop-backtester)). Dividend capture, Halloween, pre-holiday, day-of-week, lunar, expiry and January effects, and turn-of-month (file 00 "Leave"). The US index-inclusion effect imported to India. Weekly-cadence momentum variants. Vendor strategy records from Tradetron, Streak and smallcase. The open-source repos are best read as a catalogue of what failed: the positive headlines (16-25% CAGR) come from gross or lightly costed runs on 18-45 current large caps over the 2020-2025 bull market, and the two repos that tested carefully report the signal dying under costs.

## Conclusion

The sweep's most useful finding is a negative one about the whole genre: published Indian anomalies shrink when the sample turns recent. The fundamentals factors fall from t 3-4 to t 1.7-2.1 in 2015-2025, the Momentum 30 backtest-to-live gap more than halves, and the Nifty-exclusion rebound reverses sign between two papers. A pre-registered promoter-buying test should therefore be sized for an effect well below the 2006-2014 mean of +182 bp, and the +59 bp median, not the mean, is the right planning number. A second takeaway is that for slow delivery strategies the cost bar is not what kills ideas. Event counts of 3 to 40 a year, windows with four independent observations and the inability to get point-in-time data kill them first, which is why the two items that need no new data (the turnover split and the rank-band overlay) rank above several ideas with larger headline numbers.

The immediate action is not the pre-registration itself but the data decision, because the one number that decides the strategy, the return from the next open after broadcast, can only be computed from a dataset the exchange's terms currently put out of reach for automated collection. The protocol, benchmark, horizon and bars can be written down now without looking at any return; what the owner needs to settle is whether to ask NSE for permission, buy a licensed history, or export a few years manually. Until one of those happens, the zero-data measurements are the only honest next steps.
