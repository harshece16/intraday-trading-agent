## 1. Conclusion first

**Phase 2 has not found a tradable edge, and it has now measured why one is
unlikely to exist at this trade structure.**

Three findings decide it:

1. **Friction is 0.398R per trade**, not the 0.254R previously reported. The
   old figure was charges only; slippage was already hidden inside "gross".
2. **The best feature found — daily ATR% — is worth +8.11 bp per session as
   a quintile spread, against a round-trip cost of 16.60 bp.** Even the most
   concentrated version (+16.30 bp) does not clear cost.
3. **Three of ten surviving features are one factor, not three.** Controlling
   for ATR%, both 52-week-range features fall below significance.

The live journal says the same thing independently: **8 paper round trips,
gross −₹5.26, charges ₹163.45 — costs are 97% of the loss**, and the median
reward-to-risk across 8,770 scored setups is **0.050**.

**No real money has ever been at risk.** All 16 trade legs in the journal are
`mode='paper'`; the engine is in paper mode now.

**The held-out look has not been taken.** One feature qualifies for it.

## 4. Harness validation, and a correction to it

**The null test passed at −0.0495R against a ±0.05R band** — marginally; the
95% CI [−0.0616, −0.0373] straddles the band edge.

**The explanation given for that bias was wrong.** The step-1 file said
stop-first-on-same-bar and gap-through-at-open "are the expected sources".
Measured over 4,327 null trades:

| Candidate | Share of the bias |
| --- | --- |
| A single bar spanning both stop and target → stop-first | **0.00%** — 0 of 4,327; it has never once fired |
| Stops gapping through their price | 6.8% |
| Both together | 13% |
| **Unexplained by fill rules** | **87%** |

**The bias is negative intraday drift on a long-heavy book.** Longs average
**−0.0947R**, shorts **+0.0351R**, and the side mix alone reproduces the
whole figure (0.714 × −0.0947 + 0.286 × +0.0351 = −0.0576R against a measured
−0.0557R).

**This forces a correction to the headline number.** The null realised 71.4%
long while the real strategy is 59.7% long, so it overstated its own bias:

| Side mix | Implied harness bias |
| --- | --- |
| Null as realised (71.4% long) | −0.0576R |
| **Real strategy (59.7% long)** | **−0.0424R** |
| Balanced (50%) | −0.0298R |

**The bias is not a constant.** It scales with how long-heavy the book is, so
every future hypothesis must compute its own rather than inherit −0.05R.

One real asymmetry survives: **0 of 792 target exits ever filled better than
target, while 18 of 2,392 stops filled worse than their stop.** Stops can
slip; targets cannot. Pessimistic as claimed — worth 0.0038R, not 0.05R.

## 10. Carried caveats

- **Survivorship bias is worst exactly here.** Today's NIFTY 100 applied to
  history, on a volatility sort — the volatile names that fell out of the
  index are the ones missing, and the finding is that volatility predicts
  negative intraday returns.
- **Selection-sample only.** Everything has been seen by the process that
  chose it.
- **The held-out split is not pristine.** Earlier work ran against
  2026-01-02 onward; the only genuinely unseen data is after 2026-09-20, of
  which there is none.
- **The 10× journal-vs-backtest rate discrepancy is unresolved**, and the
  funnel is only as trustworthy as that.
- **Eight paper trades prove nothing on their own.** They agree with the
  495-session backtest, which is worth noting and not more.
- **A cross-sectional spread is not a strategy** — equal weight on both
  tails, no stop, no sizing, and a short leg that may not be available.
