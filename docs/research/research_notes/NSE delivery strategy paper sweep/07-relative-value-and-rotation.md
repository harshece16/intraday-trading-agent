# Relative-Value and Rotation Strategies for an NSE Delivery Retail Account (Rs 3-10 L, Zerodha)

Scope note: 30 numbered candidates (C1-C30) are catalogued across the sections below. Each candidate has a facts block under "Cited Findings" (citation, sample, effect size, t-stat or Sharpe, turnover, gross or net, replication, India evidence) and a matching line under "Inferences" (data needed, Indian cost, whether it clears, long-only version (a) versus long-cash + short-single-stock-future version (b), overlap with `00-already-covered.md`). Anything I computed myself is labelled as arithmetic or inference. WebSearch returns summarised text, so numbers taken from search summaries (CXO, Alpha Architect, Concretum, Business Standard) are second-hand and flagged. Today is 2026-11-01.

Cost anchors used throughout (from `00-already-covered.md`, as I read it): stock delivery round trip 33.05 bp at Rs 33,000 per name, 39.05 bp with doubled slippage, about 41 bp at Rs 3 L across 15 names, 56-59 bp at Rs 5.5-6.7 k per name; DP charge Rs 15.34 per scrip per sell day; ETF round trip about 10 bp in the covered turn-of-month row (the brief says 5-7 bp plus spread). Futures leg: see the cost section near the end.

## Q1. Pairs and statistical arbitrage at a daily-to-weekly horizon: what does the evidence say, and is any version runnable with delivery-only stock trading?

### Takeaway
US pairs and residual stat-arb profits were real but decayed hard after about 2000-2003 and shrink to near zero after realistic costs; the Indian evidence is a PhD thesis claiming 34% a year only for stock-futures-versus-stock-futures pairs, while spot pairs, cointegration blog tests and sector pair papers show no robust net edge. A delivery-only account cannot short, so every pair is either a long-only mean-reversion tilt (no hedge) or needs a Rs 5-20 L futures lot that is large relative to a Rs 3-10 L account.

### Cited Findings
- **C1. Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst distance pairs (US).** Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst (2006, Review of Financial Studies 19(3)); US 1962-2002; up to 11% annualised excess return (about 0.9% a month by my arithmetic); top-20 pairs lost money in 71 of 474 months; top-5 pairs earned 5.98% per 6 months on committed capital; after about 200 bp per 6 months of conservative costs the net was 88-168 bp per 6 months; a six-month trading window with pairs formed from a 12-month price-distance ranking; no t-stat or Sharpe in the summaries I could read. [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2013/06/pairs-trading-kicks-butt/), [NBER w7032](https://nber.org/papers/w7032). Replication status: sample ended in 2002, and later work (C2, C3) finds the decline below. India evidence: none for this exact design (see C6, C7).
- **C2. Rad-Low-Faff, three methods head-to-head (US).** Rad, Low & Faff (2016, Quantitative Finance), US 1962-2014, monthly returns on employed capital. Gross/net/net Sharpe/max net drawdown per [CXO Advisory's summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/best-stock-pairs-trading-method): distance 0.88% / 0.36% / 0.33 / -12%; cointegration 0.83% / 0.33% / 0.34 / -17%; copula 0.43% / 0.05% / 0.08 / -20%. The [Bond University abstract](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/the-profitability-of-pairs-trading-strategies-distance-cointegrat/) gives 91/85/43 bp gross and 38/33/5 bp net a month (small differences from CXO's rounding). Costs were time-varying: broker fees 0.7% in 1962 falling to 0.09%, price impact 0.3% before 1988 and 0.2% since, shorting cost assumed zero (CXO summary). Performance peaked around 1990 and was unattractive after about 2000; trading opportunities fell between 2007-11 and 2012-14 (distance -40%, cointegration -35%, copula -15%); the strategies work better in turbulent markets (CXO summary). Turnover and holding period per pair: not in the summaries.
- **C3. Do & Faff refined and cost-robust pairs (US).** Do & Faff (2010, Financial Analysts Journal 66(4), 83-95) find pairs profitability on a declining trend but strong in turbulence ([Bond](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/does-simple-pairs-trading-still-work/)). Do & Faff (2012, Journal of Financial Research) report about 30 bp a month risk-adjusted for well-matched pairs within refined industry groups, after commissions, market impact and short fees ([Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/41722/)). Fil (2020) reports that reasonable costs nearly eliminate excess returns in recent work and that pairs work more in emerging markets ([arXiv 2010.01157](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01157)). Huck & Afawubo find the distance method insignificant after risk and costs on S&P 500 constituents ([Applied Economics 47(6)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/applec/v47y2015i6p599-613.html), per search summary).
- **C4. Correlation-sorted "pairs momentum" (US).** Chen, Chen, Chen & Li (2019, Management Science 65(1), 370-389) form pairs from historical return correlations across CRSP and report large significant abnormal returns; keywords include pairs momentum, industry momentum and short-term reversal ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v65y2019i1p370-389.html)). A blog quotes alphas up to 36% a year (about 3% a month by arithmetic) for an equal-weighted portfolio, citing a 2012 working-paper version ([PairTradeFinder blog](https://blog.pairtradefinder.com/2019/07/)); I did not find the published-version figure, the sample years or the cost treatment.
- **C5. Avellaneda-Lee PCA and ETF-residual stat-arb (US).** Avellaneda & Lee (2010, Quantitative Finance 10(7), 761-782): PCA-based residual mean reversion, average annual Sharpe 1.44 for 1997-2007 net of transaction costs, falling to 0.9 in 2003-2007; ETF-factor version Sharpe 1.1 for 1997-2007 with degradation after 2002, rising to 1.51 for 2003-2007 when volume information is added ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v10y2010i7p761-782.html), [NYU abstract](https://cims.nyu.edu/ams/abstracts/avellaneda.html)). Long-short, so it needs shorting; holding period and turnover not captured. India evidence: none found.
- **Survey and decay.** Krauss (2017, Journal of Economic Surveys; [FAU discussion paper 09-2015](https://www.iwf.rw.fau.de/files/2016/03/09-2015.pdf)) reviews 90+ papers: cointegration and stochastic-control approaches often excel out of sample, and profitability has generally declined (secondary description only; I did not read the paper). A mixed-copula pairs write-up exists at [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/mixed-copula-pairs-trading-strategy/); I extracted no numbers from it.
- **C6. India stock-futures pairs (the one positive Indian result).** Aggarwal & Aggarwal, PhD thesis "A Study of Statistical Arbitrage Opportunities in Indian Securities Market" (Punjab Agricultural University, 2021): daily data 2011-2017, distance and cointegration pairs, Fama-French 3-factor adjusted; stock-futures pairs earn up to 34% annualised including costs (about 2.8% a month by arithmetic); stock spot pairs and spot-versus-future pairs are not profitable; currency spot-future arbitrage about 10.52% annualised ([Krishikosh record](https://krishikosh.egranth.ac.in/handle/1/5810177335), via search result). Journal version: Asia-Pacific Financial Markets 28(1), 79-99 (2021) ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/apfinm/v28y2021i1d10.1007_s10690-020-09317-1.html)); I did not confirm that the journal paper repeats the 34%. Out-of-sample after 2017: not found.
- **C7. India spot pairs and cointegration tests (weak or null).** Sen (arXiv 2211.07080): cointegration pairs in five NSE sectors, formed on 2018-2020 and tested on 2021 annual returns; auto and realty pairs did best, two of five IT pairs lost money; no Sharpe seen ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.07080)). A related 12-sector clustering version (formation 2018-2021) is on [Authorea](https://authorea.com/users/691568/articles/682160-a-cointegration-and-clustering-based-approach-to-pair-trading-of-stocks-from-selected-sectors-of-the-indian-stock-market) (unverified). ESG-screened pairs: median Sharpe near zero with wide spread ([arXiv 2401.14761](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14761)). A QuantInsti EPAT project on NSE large caps (start year given as 2015 or 2016, to 2025) reports Sharpe 0.089, annual return 0.30% (about 0.025% a month by arithmetic) and max drawdown -34.31% on a fixed, survivorship-biased universe ([IB Campus copy](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/statistical-arbitrage-using-cointegrated-stock-pairs-in-indian-equity-market-2015-2025-epat-project/)); that project assumed 5 bp per leg per side.
- **C8. Nifty vs Bank Nifty or index-vs-constituent spread.** I found no peer-reviewed paper that tests a daily-to-weekly Nifty/Bank Nifty spread. An IIM Bangalore thesis (Kumar, 2011) studies Nifty vs Nifty futures cointegration and says the strategy beat holding the index, with no numbers seen ([IIMB repository](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/handle/2074/18231)); [Tradejini](https://www.tradejini.com/blogs/pair-trading-made-simple-with-a-clear-look-at-statistical-arbitrage-in-india) is a non-academic explainer with no results.
- **C9. Long-only half of a pair.** No study isolates the long-only leg in India or the US in the sources I read; the literature above is long-short only.

### Inferences
- **C1 (GGR).** Data: daily adjusted closes for a liquid NSE universe (daily NSE bhavcopy suffices). Cost: a 2-leg pair is 2 round trips in cash, about 66-82 bp, at the covered delivery costs; against a US net of 88-168 bp per 6 months that uses 200 bp costs, India's per-trip cost sits at or above the US assumption, and the US edge was gone after about 2000. (a) long-only: loses the hedge, becomes a loser-bounce tilt, overlapping the closed short-term-reversal rows. (b) cash + short SSF: about 33-41 bp cash + about 5 bp futures STT + fees + roll. Clears cost: no, by the post-2000 decay and 2x-the-assumed-cost Indian frictions. Overlap: covered "Pairs: Leave" and short-term reversal rejected (trials 121-122).
- **C2 (RLF).** Net 33-36 bp a month on employed capital is the best published net figure, from a sample whose later years are weaker; Indian round-trip costs of about 41 bp per trade exceed the modern US cost assumption (0.2% impact + 9 bp commission), so the net would likely be around zero or negative. Same (a)/(b) logic as C1. Clears cost: no. Copula variant: 5 bp net in the US, a clear no. Overlap: "Pairs: Leave".
- **C3 (Do-Faff).** The 30 bp a month survives only within refined industry groups with low costs and available shorting; an unhedged India long leg cannot replicate it. Clears: no.
- **C4 (Chen).** Correlation-pair "momentum" is closely related to short-term reversal and industry momentum; the 36% figure is from a working paper and is long-short. Treat as overlapping closed reversal work. Clears: unproven.
- **C5 (Avellaneda-Lee).** Sharpe 0.9-1.44 with high turnover and shorting; at about 41 bp per Indian delivery trip a daily-to-weekly residual-reversion book is cost-dominated, and the strategy is a variant of short-term reversal (closed). Clears: no.
- **C6 (India stock-futures pairs).** Both legs are single-stock futures, so one pair needs two lots (about Rs 10-40 L notional at Rs 5-20 L per lot) plus SPAN margin (not found); a Rs 3-10 L account cannot size it. Costs: 5 bp STT on each sell-side futures leg (post-April 2026 STT, news-reported) plus roll, which is much cheaper per notional than delivery (about 10-15 bp round trip against 41 bp), but the thesis's 34% predates the 2026 STT hike and rests on one unreplicated, unpublished-in-full sample. Data: futures daily settle prices, not in plain NSE equity data. Overlap: "Pairs: Leave"; closed under the covered mandate if futures are not allowed. Clears: only on paper; capital-infeasible.
- **C7 (India spot pairs).** The consistent Indian spot result (Aggarwal spot pairs not profitable; EPAT Sharpe 0.089; ESG median Sharpe near zero) points the same way as the US decay: no net edge at delivery cost. Clears: no.
- **C8 (Nifty/Bank Nifty).** In delivery form this is NIFTYBEES versus BANKBEES long-only rotation: no short leg, so it is just a relative-strength switch (covered "Sector rotation: Leave"). The index-futures hedge version needs two index lots (sizes not sourced here). No evidence base to size an edge. Clears: unknown, no evidence.
- **C9 (long-only half).** Inference only: buying the temporarily cheap leg with no hedge adds a market beta of about 1 and a single delivery round trip of about 33-41 bp; the pair's edge is the spread, which this removes the ability to isolate. I would not propose it as a candidate on this evidence.

### Gaps
- Chen et al. published-version alpha, sample years and cost treatment (only a blog quote of a working-paper figure found).
- Avellaneda-Lee holding period and turnover; Gatev et al. t-stats and Sharpe.
- Aggarwal thesis: costs assumed, number of trades, whether 34% survives the 2026 STT regime; sample of pairs; whether the 34% is on capital or on notional.
- Sen paper net-of-cost result and Sharpe (the PDF was unreadable through the fetch tool); Authorea paper unverified.
- A Canadian pairs replication and a China/Hong Kong post-2012 decay paper were mentioned in search summaries; I did not retain their sources, so they are not used.
- Any Nifty/Bank Nifty spread study with numbers.
- Actual SPAN plus exposure margin for single-stock futures under the 2026 regime (no reliable page found).

## Q2. Sector and industry rotation: what is the evidence, especially on NSE?

### Takeaway
US industry momentum is real but short-lived and largely subsumed by factor momentum; India has two sector-momentum papers with no usable net numbers and no backtest of an NSE sector-ETF rotation with a CAGR. The covered file already says "Leave" for sector rotation, and the Pivot-B list names it as untested (one trial).

### Cited Findings
- **C10. Industry momentum (US).** Moskowitz & Grinblatt (1999, Journal of Finance 54(4), 1249-1290): industry momentum is strongest at the 1-month horizon, lasts up to a year, and explains most individual-stock momentum except the 12-month version ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v54y1999i4p1249-1290.html), [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Do-Industries-Explain-Momentum)). An Alpha Architect summary gives 1-month industry momentum about 8.7% annualised (about 0.72% a month by arithmetic), t = 4.1, and says it is largely a by-product of factor momentum ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-explanatory-power-of-factor-momentum/)). A risk-managed version earns 33-36 bp a month ([Grobys & Kolari 2019](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstream/10024/11757/2/Osuva_Grobys_Kolari_2019.pdf)). Turnover is high at 1-month formation and holding (monthly rebalancing). Gross of costs.
- **C11. NSE sector-index momentum (India).** Narayan, Ahmed, Sharma & Prabheesh (2014, Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 30, 44-61): 50 NSE stocks in six sectors, Jan 2001-Dec 2012, momentum, MA and filter rules with long winners and short losers inside each sector, 1/3/6-month holds; IT is the most profitable sector, pharma the least; the authors flag endogeneity and heteroskedasticity and call their regressions doubtful; no numeric returns or net-of-cost result in the digest ([CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2015/03/how-profitable-is-the-indian-stock-market-digest-summary)). Sehgal & Jain (2011, Journal of Advances in Management Research 8(1), 99-122): momentum in Indian stock and sectoral returns, stronger for 6-6 than 12-12; a sectoral momentum factor captures part of the stock momentum profit and CAPM and FF3 do not; the abstract gives no sample, numbers or costs ([Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/09727981111129327/full/html)). Loose stock-level analogue: Nifty200 Momentum 30 variants had net CAGR of about 8.5-19.4% against 10.4% for Nifty 50 over 19 years ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/is-indian-momentum-investing-just-a-liquidity-illusion/)).
- **C12. Sector ETF rotation top-3 (practitioner).** Quantpedia's sector momentum rotational system picks the top 3 of 12-month sector momentum with a 1-month hold ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/sector-momentum-rotational-system)); a more recent Quantpedia piece tested nine Select Sector SPDRs 1998-June 2026 with a calendar-timing overlay of Sharpe about 0.55 and long-short 5.99% a year, with no turnover number ([Quantpedia tag page](https://quantpedia.com/tag/sector-momentum/)). Indian practitioner posts (e.g., [MNCL](https://www.mnclgroup.com/sectoral-momentum-how-to-track-rotational-trends-in-indian-markets)) describe sector rotation with no audited results. I found no Indian sector-rotation backtest with CAGR and drawdown.

### Inferences
- **C10 (MG).** Needs shorting for the original long-short; the long-only winner-industry half is a momentum tilt, which sits inside the covered momentum family (bar t >= 2.865; family N = 11). Data: industry classifications and daily prices; daily NSE bhavcopy suffices. Cost: monthly rotation of a basket of stocks at 33-41 bp per round trip is a 400-500 bp annual drag at full monthly turnover (arithmetic) against a 8.7% gross long-short figure that is mostly subsumed by factor momentum. Clears: no, at stock level.
- **C11 (NSE sector index).** Only runnable through sector ETFs or index funds, and without net numbers there is nothing to size. Overlap: covered "Sector rotation: Leave".
- **C12 (sector ETF rotation).** Cost per switch is the ETF 5-10 bp plus spread, which is favourable, so this is the only form of sector rotation that could clear cost at monthly turnover. The binding unknown is sector-ETF liquidity and spreads on NSE (not found). (a) is the natural form; (b) not applicable. Clears: unknown, testable cheaply with NSE sector index TRI data as a proxy and with real ETF prices for costs. Overlap: Pivot-B names sector rotation as untested (1 trial); the covered file says "Leave".

### Gaps
- NSE sectoral ETF average daily turnover and quoted spread (Nippon and Dhan pages only had undated snapshots).
- Indian sector-rotation backtest with net returns; Narayan et al. and Sehgal & Jain numerical results.
- Alpha Architect's 8.7% figure is a secondary summary; the original table was not seen.

## Q3. Multi-asset rotation with NSE-listed ETFs (equity, gold, silver, liquid/debt, international): dual momentum, tactical allocation, permanent portfolio, risk parity?

### Takeaway
Dual momentum and trend-following allocation have the best published US evidence (Sharpe about 0.8-0.9 in sample) but the Indian evidence is blogs only, with CAGR gains over Nifty ranging from about 0.04% a month (Capitalmind) to 0.55% a month (MomentumLab, weak source) and costs and taxes unstated. The covered file classes dual momentum as "allocation, not excess; runnable at Rs 5,500", so the mandate question is whether a drawdown-reduction allocation is worth a pre-registered trial, not whether it beats the index in return.

### Cited Findings
- **C13. Antonacci Global Equity Momentum (US/global).** 1974-2013: about 17.43% a year, 12.64% volatility, Sharpe 0.87, max drawdown 22.7%, versus 9.55% for ACWI (about 0.66% a month excess by arithmetic), gross of costs, monthly rebalancing ([CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/a-few-notes-on-dual-momentum-investing)); a Seeking Alpha version to October 2015 shows 17.36%, Sharpe 0.89 and max drawdown -17.84% ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/article/3768586-why-does-dual-momentum-outperform)). Max-drawdown figures differ between the two sources above (22.7% against 17.84%, different end dates). ReSolve's replication (2019) finds attractive absolute and risk-adjusted performance since 1950 in and out of sample (out-of-sample windows 1950-1973 and 2012-2018) but says the original specification looks lucky against alternative lookbacks ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/articles/2019/05/06/global-equity-momentum-a-craftsmans-perspective)). A 2026 Polish guide gives a rolling 12-month Sharpe of about 0.55 for GEM versus 0.65 for S&P buy-and-hold over 2014-2024, with no primary citation, so treat as unverified ([Freenance](https://freenance.io/strategie/dual-momentum-gary-antonacci-2026-pl-strategia-momentum-relative-absolute-implementacja-przyklad/)).
- **C14. Nifty-Gold dual momentum (India; priority candidate).** Three blog backtests, none peer-reviewed. (i) [Samasthiti](https://samasthiti.substack.com/p/indian-edition-of-the-dual-momentum): Dec 1996-Jan 2024, month-end data, 12-month lookback, monthly rebalance, Nifty/Gold; CAGR 14.0% against Nifty 12.4% (+1.6% a year, about 0.13% a month by arithmetic); in Nov 2008 Nifty fell more than 50% against about 21% for the strategy; 2020 about 30% against about 11%; gold priced from INR spot before 30 Nov 2009 and GOLDBEES after; costs and taxes not stated. (ii) [Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2022/06/should-gold-be-in-your-long-term-portfolio/): Rs 1 L from 2005 grows to Rs 7.7 L for Nifty buy-and-hold and Rs 8.4 L for Nifty-gold dual momentum, 23 trades in 17 years, monthly review, switch to the higher 12-month return; by my arithmetic that is about 12.8% against 13.3% a year, a gap of roughly 0.04% a month; the same piece says gold trailed Nifty TRI by 2.1% a year from 1999 to June 2022 and beat Nifty in 34% of 5-year and 39% of 10-year rolling windows (tables were images, so not read). (iii) A [TradingQnA thread](https://tradingqna.com/t/dual-momentum-strategy-with-niftybees-goldbees/174470) reports a ChatGPT-run 14-year NIFTYBEES/GOLDBEES test at 16.06% CAGR and notes that NIFTYBEES + GOLDBEES buy-and-hold returns a similar number (unverified).
- **C15. Nifty / Gold / fixed-income dual momentum with a cash leg.** [MomentumLab](https://momentumlab.substack.com/p/backtesting-dual-momentum-strategy): 2014-2024, 200-day lookback, Nifty/Gold/FD; CAGR 18.79% against Nifty 12.13% and gold 10.86% (a gap of about 0.55% a month by arithmetic); max drawdown -11.42% against -34.00% for Nifty and -21.44% for gold; rebalance frequency, costs and instruments unspecified, so it is a weak source.
- **C16. Faber trend-following GTAA on ETFs.** Faber (2007), 10-month SMA, 1972-2005: Sharpe 0.81, CAGR 11.7%, max drawdown 9.5% (monthly) ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/global-tactical-asset-allocation)); "Revisited 10 Years Later" (J. Portfolio Management 2018) reports the Sharpe rising from 0.45 to 0.80 ([etf.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist)); a 2003-2016 review found a Sharpe of 0.87 against 0.60 for buy-and-hold ([Capital Spectator](https://capitalspectator.com/?p=7974)). CXO flags data-snooping on the 10-month window ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/long-term-outperformance-from-trends-defined-by-moving-averages)). India: no Faber replication on NIFTYBEES/GOLDBEES found.
- **C17. Freefincal tactical entry/exit rules (India).** The 10-month moving-average rule on Sensex with an assumed 6% post-tax fixed-income leg, rolling 10-year windows, about 5 equity trades in 10 years, final value cut 20% for exit load and tax; the author says it "almost always lowers risk", with no numeric CAGR or drawdown in the text, and that tactical timing needs more discipline than an SIP ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/?p=24910)). A separate Freefincal test of a 6-month versus 12-month MA rule on the Nifty Midcap 150 Momentum 50 TRI says results "seem promising", with no numbers extracted ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/momentum-index-fund-will-tactical-entry-exit-give-better-results/)).
- **C18. Permanent portfolio, static gold-equity mix and risk parity (India).** Freefincal's 25% each in stocks (Sensex TRI), liquid fund, gold (INR) and I-BEX gilts, Sep 1996-Apr 2020, monthly rebalancing (annual enough for individuals); no CAGR or drawdown in the text (charts only); notes tax inefficiency in the debt-fund version; an equity-oriented variant uses 25% equity, 40% arbitrage funds, 25% gold, 5% gilts, 5% cash (Oct 2005-Apr 2020) ([Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/this-portfolio-works-in-all-market-conditions-will-you-invest/)). US benchmark: Browne permanent portfolio CAGR 8.14% and max drawdown -13.19% since 1972 ([DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/harry-browne-permanent-portfolio)). Gold as a hedge or safe haven in India is mixed: Shahani & Bansal (IIM Calcutta, 2021, 2008-2019) find OLS shows no safe haven, quantile regression shows hedge properties at some quantiles and lower-tail safe-haven behaviour for some pairs ([IIM Calcutta repository](https://ir.iimcal.ac.in:8443/jspui/handle/123456789/3345)); Dey & Sampath (IIM Kozhikode) find spot gold an effective hedge against stock prices and a safe haven in the 2007-09 crisis ([working paper](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:iik:wpaper:251)); Majumder (2022) concludes neither gold nor crypto is a safe haven ([IGDR](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/igdrpp/igdr-10-2021-0131.html)). No Indian risk-parity net-of-tax numbers found.
- **C19. LIQUIDBEES (and liquid ETFs) as the cash leg.** LIQUIDBEES keeps its price near flat and pays daily dividends as units; those dividends are taxed at slab since DDT was removed ([Zerodha support on DDT](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/ddt-on-debt-funds)); Capitalmind says liquid funds are better for most uses and LIQUIDBEES is better for margin pledging ([Capitalmind 2016](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2016/04/premium-understanding-liquid-bees-an-etf-for-safe-parking-of-funds-and-to-optimize-margins/)).

### Inferences
- **C13 (GEM).** Indian analogue is NIFTYBEES vs an international ETF vs a bond/liquid ETF; the international leg is distorted by creation freezes (C24), so GEM itself is not runnable as published. Annual dual-momentum effect of about 0.66% a month is in-sample, gross and US-global. Overlap: covered "Dual momentum: allocation, not excess; Leave".
- **C14 (Nifty-Gold dual momentum; priority).** Data: NIFTYBEES and GOLDBEES (or NSE gold price) adjusted daily closes; daily NSE data plus ETF prices suffice. Cost: Capitalmind's 23 trades in 17 years is about 1.4 switches a year at 5-10 bp per leg, about 15-30 bp a year of friction, so costs are trivial. The bigger drag is tax: every switch realises a gain on the sold ETF (equity ETF STCG 20% if under 12 months, 12.5% LTCG over Rs 1.25 L; gold ETF slab if under 12 months, 12.5% after 12), which buy-and-hold defers (the tax numbers are in Q7). The effect size in the three blogs is 0.04% to 0.55% a month, and the longest and most careful sample (Samasthiti, 27 years) is 0.13% a month, with gains concentrated in two events (2008, 2020), so the evidence is drawdown reduction, not excess return. Clears cost: yes on friction, unproven after tax; excess-return t-stat not available, so it does not meet the covered momentum bar (t >= 2.865) as an alpha claim. (a) is the only form; (b) not applicable. Overlap: "Leave (allocation, not excess); runnable at Rs 5,500", and Pivot-B names gold/debt dual momentum as an untested family (1 trial). If the project wants the trial, it must be pre-registered as a drawdown-reduction claim, not an excess-return claim.

## Q4. Cash-futures basis: can a single-stock-future basis predict spot, or can cash-and-carry beat the risk-free rate?

### Takeaway
Cash-and-carry returns in India are thin, about the liquid-fund yield net of frictions, and the pricing-error predictor works for the same day only. The 2026 STT hike (to 5 bp on futures sales) makes the carry leg more expensive than the brief's 1 bp figure.

### Cited Findings
- **C20. Cash-and-carry on single-stock futures.** Practitioner estimate: retail costs about 0.20-0.40% of notional per round trip, example net 0.40-0.60% a month (illustrative), and the hurdle is a liquid-fund yield of about 6-7%; arbitrage funds track money-market rates; the strategy needs deliverable single stocks, not index futures ([OneTradeJournal](https://onetradejournal.com/strategies/cash-and-carry-arbitrage), [MarketCalls on arbitrage funds](https://www.marketcalls.in/investment/demystifying-arbitrage-funds-a-complete-guide-for-indian-investors.html)). Academic: stock futures mispricing is significant on NSE, mostly underpricing, with arbitrageurs constrained by short-selling restrictions (Nandan, Agrawal & Jindal, Indian Journal of Finance 2015) ([i-Scholar](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/ijf/article/view/202517)); an NSE research paper reports mispricing shrinking with days to expiry ([NSE paper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/res_paper_final185.pdf), per search excerpt, not opened).
- **Futures STT after Budget 2026-27.** STT on futures was raised from 0.02% to 0.05%, effective around 1 April 2026 ([Swastika](https://www.swastika.co.in/big-budget-day/stt-hiked-on-futures-options-union-budget-2026-key-points), [Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/fm-sitharaman-hikes-stt-on-futures-to-005-pc-in-budget-2026)); these are news and brokerage pages, not the Finance Act text.
- **C21. SSF pricing error as a spot predictor.** Pathak, Verousis & Chauhan (2017, Journal of Emerging Market Finance 16(2), 169-187): portfolios sorted on the single-stock-futures cost-of-carry pricing error carry incremental information about day-t returns and not beyond; consistent only for liquid stocks ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/emffin/v16y2017i2p169-187.html)). Sample, N and returns not in the abstract.
- **C22. Index futures leading spot (daily).** Chang (2011, Applied Financial Economics 21(10), 755-761): Indian index futures lead spot; spot does not lead futures ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apfiec/v21y2011i10p755-761.html)). Bearing: the lead is intraday-scale in most such studies (not shown in this abstract).
- **C23. Roll-week and expiry effects.** A 2025 Review of Derivatives Research paper finds volume and volatility rose after the expiry-day move ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/revdev/v28y2025i3d10.1007_s11147-025-09221-8.html)); a 2025 arXiv SIP paper finds expiry-day SIPs beat first-day SIPs by 0.5-2.5% a year ([arXiv 2507.04859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04859)). No dedicated roll-week return study was found.
- **Lot sizes.** SEBI's October 2024 circular sets a Rs 15 lakh minimum contract value at introduction and a band of Rs 15-20 lakh for single stocks with half-yearly recalibration ([Oquilia](https://www.oquilia.com/news/nse-fo-lot-size-revision-15-lakh-contract)). Specific 2026 lot sizes conflict across pages (RELIANCE 500 vs 250; HDFCBANK 650 vs 550) ([Kotak Neo Reliance](https://www.kotakneo.com/futures-and-options/stocks-futures/reliance-industries-futures/?expiry=NOV), [Choice India](https://choiceindia.com/future-and-option/reliance-industries-ltd)).

### Inferences
- **C20 (cash-and-carry).** (b) is the only form (long cash + short future on the same stock). Data: spot, futures settle and days to expiry; daily NSE data plus F&O bhavcopy. Cost: 33-41 bp cash round trip + about 5 bp futures STT on the short leg + fees, versus an illustrative gross of 0.40-0.60% a month; since the cash leg is held to expiry and the delivery cost is paid once per roll, a monthly cycle gives a net near zero to slightly positive, and a liquid fund at 6-7% a year (about 0.5-0.6% a month) pays about the same with no lot constraint. Rolling less often than monthly (holding the cash leg across several expiries) is the only way to amortise the delivery cost. A Rs 15 L lot exceeds a Rs 3-10 L account, so it is capital-infeasible for most of the stated range. Clears: no on the evidence. Overlap: not named in the covered file's section 1 as far as my notes show; it is a new family and a negative one.
- **C21 (SSF predictor).** Same-day only, so it cannot be traded at a daily-to-weekly horizon, and it needs futures data. Clears: no (negative result).
- **C22 (index futures lead).** The covered file closes the overnight ETF premium and intraday index timing; a daily horizon version has no evidence of a lead beyond the same day. Overlap: closed lines L51/L32 and "GIFT Nifty implied open: skipped". Clears: no evidence.
- **C23 (roll-week and expiry).** The covered file marks expiry-day effects "Out of mandate". The 0.5-2.5% a year SIP finding is a timing preference for an investor who invests anyway, not a new trade. Clears: not testable as a standalone edge.

### Gaps
- SSF margin requirements (SPAN + exposure) under the 2026 regime, and current stock-future lot sizes with a reliable source.
- Primary-source text for the 0.05% futures STT.
- Dated cash-and-carry returns in India after the 2026 STT hike; arbitrage-fund tax treatment (equity-oriented?) is not verified here.
- Vipul (Vikalpa 2005, 2002-04 NSE mispricing exceeded costs) is known only from a search summary; no link retained.
- Mahalwala (2016) and Wats (2017) expiry studies are known only from search summaries (no effect and an effect over the expiry week, respectively); no links retained.
- Futures P&L tax treatment (business income) for retail was not sourced.

## Q5. ETF premium/discount and creation-unit effects: international ETFs, gold and silver ETFs, liquid ETFs

### Takeaway
Premiums on creation-frozen ETFs are real, large (up to about 12% for MON100 in 2022; a single unverified snapshot of +26.7% in June 2026; 5-9% for silver ETFs in October 2025) and a retail cost to avoid rather than a harvestable return, because retail cannot create or redeem and cannot hold a short beyond the day.

### Cited Findings
- **C24. International ETFs (MON100, MAFANG, HNGSNGBEES).** MON100 traded about 12% above iNAV on 25 May 2022 (price near 99 against iNAV near 87), with forum reports of 10-14% premiums ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/premiums-and-discounts-in-international-etfs/131293)); Kite shows a "no market-making" nudge on MON100 ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/kite-nudge-for-mon100-etf-inav/130815)); AMCs (Edelweiss, PGIM, Franklin) paused international SIPs in July 2026 as the overseas cap bound ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/mutual-funds/story/why-are-mutual-funds-stopping-international-sips-heres-how-sebis-overseas-investment-cap-works-543752-2026-07-18), [Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/international-sip-halts-etf-premiums-cmsdi5bfy03dg0zoykviv7m0w)). SEBI's overseas limit: industry-wide $7 bn, ETF sub-limit $1 bn, $300 m per ETF, per search summaries (see [Vested](https://vestedfinance.com/sebi-limits-on-overseas-investment-by-mutual-funds/)). Mechanism: market makers create or redeem units to keep price near NAV; once creation stops, premiums persist.
- **C25. Gold and silver ETF premiums.** SILVERBEES closed at Rs 156 against an iNAV of Rs 148.3 (+5.5%) on 9 October 2025, with reports of up to 9% and 5-12% for other silver ETFs ([Business Standard 9 Oct](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/silver-etfs-trade-at-steep-premiums-amid-global-supply-squeeze-125100901276_1.html), [Business Standard 5-12% risk](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/paying-too-much-silver-etfs-look-shiny-but-mind-the-5-12-premium-risk-125101300184_1.html)); AMCs froze subscriptions from 14 October ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/why-mfs-are-freezing-silver-etf-investments-and-what-it-means-for-you-125101400231_1.html)); by 17 October SILVERBEES was at Rs 160.6, about 0.7% below NAV, with discounts of up to about 3% for some ETFs, so the premium collapsed in about a week ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/the-balloon-lands-silver-s-hot-air-premiums-collapse-as-etf-frenzy-cools-125101900389_1.html)).
- **C26. Premium/flow predictability (US).** Petajisto (2017, Financial Analysts Journal): US ETF premium volatility 49 bp, average premium 6 bp; bands of 100-200 bp for international or illiquid holdings; reported profits of about 7% before costs, concentrated in international ETFs; US 2007-2014, costs excluded ([CFA Institute RPC](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2017/inefficiencies-in-the-pricing-of-exchange-traded-funds)). Brown, Davies & Ringgenberg (2021, Review of Finance 25(4), 937-972): shorting high-flow ETFs and buying low-flow ETFs earns 1.1-2.0% a month ([Arizona](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/etf-arbitrage-non-fundamental-demand-and-return-predictability/)).

### Inferences
- **C24.** A repeatable net-of-cost premium effect for retail was not found; the premium is a cost for buyers. The only retail-side use is avoidance (do not buy MON100-type ETFs at a premium) and, for an existing holder, selling into a premium. Data: iNAV is published intraday by NSE; a premium series needs iNAV history. (a) long-only: the long is the cost; (b) not applicable (ETF shorts are intraday only, and a premium that persists for months cannot be shorted intraday). Overlap: the covered file closes only the NIFTYBEES overnight premium; this is a different instrument family, but the finding is negative.
- **C25.** The silver episode reverted in about 8 days, which is longer than an intraday short and shorter than a creation-limited regime, so a swing-short is impossible for a delivery account and a premium-fade long does not exist. Avoidance only. Gold ETF premiums are small (tens of bp), which is inside the ETF round-trip cost. Clears: no.
- **C26.** Both US results need shorting and fund-level data; Petajisto's profits were concentrated in international ETFs, which in India are the creation-frozen ones where retail cannot arbitrage. Clears: no.

### Gaps
- A dated, continuous premium series for MON100, MAFANG, HNGSNGBEES, GOLDBEES and SILVERBEES.
- The "+26.74% June 2026" MON100 premium (NAV $2.88 vs close $3.65) is a single snapshot whose page I did not retain; unverified.

## Q6. Lead-lag effects: large-cap to small-cap, index to constituents, ADR/GDR, US and GIFT/SGX to Nifty

### Takeaway

ADR/GDR to Indian spot.** A Mint 2026 article says the ICICI Bank ADR premium of about 9% converged to parity and that fair value is now set on Indian exchanges ([Mint via Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Mint-Anubhav-Ghosh-Online.pdf)); an IIM Calcutta working paper studies ADR efficiency ([IIMC](https://ir.iimcal.ac.in:8443/jspui/handle/123456789/488)).

## Q7. Indian tax and cost mechanics for ETF switching and for the futures leg

### Takeaway
Equity-oriented ETFs pay 20% STCG under 12 months and 12.5% LTCG above Rs 1.25 L; gold and silver ETFs pay 12.5% LTCG after 12 months with no exemption; debt and liquid ETFs bought on or after 1 April 2023 are always short-term at slab. The tax treatment of international ETFs like MON100 is conflicted in the sources and needs verification against the scheme document. Switching between ETFs triggers realisation every time.

### Cited Findings
- Equity ETF: STCG 20% (Section 111A, sales on or after 23 July 2024), LTCG 12.5% above Rs 1.25 L a year (Section 112A), holding period over 12 months. Gold and silver ETFs (listed): over 12 months, LTCG 12.5% without indexation and no mention of the Rs 1.25 L exemption, STCG at slab. International ETFs: the same page's table lists 12 months, STCG at slab, LTCG 12.5%, but its text says an overseas equity ETF "does not become an equity-oriented fund merely because it holds shares abroad", that gold, silver and international ETFs "can fall under the 'other mutual funds' route", and that MON100 is not named so the scheme's own tax disclosure should be checked. Debt and liquid ETFs bought on or after 1 April 2023: gains deemed short-term under Section 50AA, taxed at slab ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/etf-taxation-india/)). Consistent with [Zerodha Fund House](https://www.zerodhafundhouse.com/blog/understanding-etf-taxation-in-india-a-simplified-guide-in-2025/) and [Business Standard on Budget 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/budget-2024-brings-relief-for-gold-etfs-equity-fofs-international-schemes-124072301258_1.html) on the 12-month gold ETF holding period (the Business Standard page returned 403 to my fetch, so I used search-level text). Some other guides give 24 months for gold and silver; I could not resolve the conflict.
- Liquid ETF dividends are taxed at slab since DDT was removed ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/console/reports/taxation/articles/ddt-on-debt-funds)).
- Futures STT: see Q4 (0.05% on futures, effective about 1 April 2026).

### Inferences
- Because switching is a sale and a purchase, a rotation between ETFs realises gains each time, whereas buy-and-hold defers; with 1-2 switches a year the tax drag is the main cost of C14-C17, not the 5-10 bp friction.
- Nifty to LIQUIDBEES parking converts the equity gain at the equity rate (20% or 12.5%) and then earns slab-taxed carry. For a taxpayer in a high slab the liquid ETF carry is taxed at slab plus cess (and surcharge where it applies), which is worse than equity-rate treatment if an arbitrage fund is taxed as equity-oriented (inference; arbitrage-fund tax not verified here).
- For the futures leg: STT 5 bp on the sell side, brokerage and exchange fees (amounts not sourced here), plus a roll that repeats the leg each month, so a 12-month futures hedge pays the leg about 12 times.

### Gaps
- Primary-source classification and holding period for MON100/MAFANG (Income Tax Act sections, scheme tax note).
- Whether gold/silver ETF long-term is 12 or 24 months in every source; whether any 2025-26 amendment changed debt-ETF treatment.
- Tax treatment of futures P&L for a retail trader (not sourced), and arbitrage-fund taxation.
