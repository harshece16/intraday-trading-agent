# Gap sweep: analyst revisions, credit ratings, OFS/disinvestment/PSU and news sentiment as NSE delivery signals (days to months)

Written 2026-11-01. Tags: [P] read in a primary source (paper page, PDF text, or my own probe), [S] secondary (search summary, news, blog, abstract seen only via a listing), [C] my arithmetic. Cost hurdle used throughout: about 35-40 bp round trip for delivery (covered file: 33.05 bp at Rs 33,000 per trade, 39.05 bp with doubled slippage, 56.1 bp at Rs 5,500, DP Rs 15.34 per sell). Reference bar for a new idea: the covered file's momentum-family bar t >= 2.865 (N=12); I did not establish the bar for a brand-new family, so 2.865 is used as a conservative stand-in. A delivery book cannot short, so long-short US results count only their long leg here.

## 1. Analyst recommendation changes and target-price revisions

### Takeaway
Indian evidence is a handful of small event studies (36 to ~1,200 calls) with abnormal returns mostly on the publication day and no out-of-sample test; the one paper I read in full (BSE-100, 36 Buy calls) concludes the day-0 gain does not persist. No free source with a continuous, dated recommendation or target-price history was found, so the signal cannot be built from public data at the reader's budget.

### Cited Findings
- Sharda (2022), VILAKSHAN XIMB Journal of Management 19(1):2-19: 36 Buy recommendations on S&P BSE 100 stocks from Business Standard, Apr 2019-Aug 2020; market model, 89-day estimation window, event window -20..+20. Day-0 AAR +0.824% (82 bp, t 2.12) [P]; day-9 AAR +1.233% (t 3.17) and day-19 AAR +1.243% (t 3.20) [P]; CAAR -6.35% at day 0 rising to +0.82% at day 20, so the pre-event drift is negative [P]. The day-0 effect is not significant under the adjusted BMP test; the author concludes the market is semi-strong efficient and the day-0 gain does not persist; day 9 is read as delayed adjustment. No Sell calls, no size split. Gross, no costs, no out-of-sample test. — [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/XJM-12-2020-0239/full/html)
- Moulya and Mallikarjunappa, South Asian Journal of Management 27(1), Nifty 50 recommendations 2012-18: no significant announcement-day reaction; a delayed response/drift exists for bullish calls only [S, search summary of the listing; abstract page not opened]. — [i-scholar listing](https://mail.i-scholar.in/index.php/sajm/article/view/195997)
- Joshi and Parmar (SSRN 3392263): 1,178 calls on 361 companies from The Economic Times print; sell calls had higher AAR than buy or hold calls [S, abstract only, SSRN returned 403; conference paper, not shown as peer-reviewed]. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3392263)
- Kadam and Sethi (2024), Cogent Economics & Finance 12(1):2423261: analysts achieved 63% of 12-month target prices; more optimistic targets mean larger error; weaker for loss-making and capital-intensive firms; caution advised on small caps. Sample size not stated in the abstract [P abstract]. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/oaefxx/v12y2024i1p2423261.html)
- Chatterjee, Kumar and Chatterjee (2020), IIMB Management Review 32(2): survival analysis of time to reach analysts' target prices (technical and fundamental calls); conclusion reported as post-recommendation market movement, not analyst insight, drives price [S, search summary; paper page returned 403/redirect]. — [IIMB IMR issue page](https://www.iimb.ac.in/imr/previous-issues/time-june-2020.php)

### Inferences
- Sharda's CAAR path (-7.2 pp over the 20 days before day 0, then +7.2 pp back by day 20 [C: -6.35 - 0.824 and 0.82 + 6.35]) in a sample period (Apr 2019-Aug 2020) that includes the Feb-Mar 2020 crash [C] looks like crash-and-recovery contaminating a market-model estimate rather than a call-driven drift. Single-day AARs of 1.2% on days 9 and 19 with only 36 events point to calendar clustering. I would not size anything off it.
- The only quantified Indian number that could matter, 82 bp on publication day, is gross, is earned between the prior close and the day-0 close, and is not capturable by an investor who reads the call after publication. After day 0 the paper itself finds no persistent gain.
- Statistical power is the binding limit, not cost: the minimum detectable mean effect at t = 2.865 is 2.865 x sigma / sqrt(N) [C]: 0.48 sigma at N = 36, 0.29 sigma at N = 100. Samples of 36 to ~1,200 hand-collected calls cannot be rebuilt without a data feed the reader does not have.
- The conflicting sign evidence (Joshi-Parmar: sell calls beat buy calls in AAR; Sharda: buy calls positive; Moulya-Mallikarjunappa: bullish drift only) means the Indian literature has no stable direction to trade.

- A foreign-brokerage sell-call study (100 NSE/BSE firms, to Mar 2016; negative and significant AAR for mid caps, positive and significant for small caps) and a Kumar-Chaturvedula-Rastogi-Bang (2009, IUP J. NSE does not publish RA recommendations that I could find. Whether any of these exposes a downloadable history was not tested (no logins, no scraping done).

## 2. Credit-rating changes (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings)

### Takeaway
Indian rating-change event studies disagree (one 167-event study finds nothing significant; one 2009-2019 NSE study reports significant negative reactions to downgrades only), and none gives a verified bp number I could read. The usable asset is a dated, machine-readable event list: NSE's "Credit Rating" corporate filings (hundreds to about 1,500 equity-universe filings a year), but direction (upgrade, downgrade, reaffirm) is not in the feed and needs PDF/XBRL parsing.

### Cited Findings
- Archana, Jayanna and Hiremath (2015), Indian Journal of Research in Capital Markets 2(4):20-32: 167 rating-change announcements from four agencies, 1991-2015; average abnormal returns around the events were statistically insignificant under t-tests, a run test indicated randomness, and the authors conclude upgrades and downgrades had "very minimal impact" on Indian stock prices. No CAR values, no upgrade/downgrade split in the abstract [P abstract]. — [IJRCM](https://www.indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/ijrcm/article/view/102634)
- "Does Credit Rating Revisions Affect the Price of Common Stock: A Study of Indian Capital Market": CRISIL/ICRA/CARE actions on NSE stocks, 2009-2019; downgrades produce significant negative abnormal returns (strongest in metals), upgrades insignificant [S, from a listing/search summary; the full text returned 403; no numbers]. — [Christ University archive](https://archives.christuniversity.in/items/show/14306)
- IL&FS episode: ICRA cut IL&FS from AA+ to BB on Sunday 2018-09-09; group stocks fell up to 14% on 2018-09-10 (IL&FS Transportation -13.79%, IL&FS Investment Managers -10.99%) [S]. — [Business Standard, 10 Sep 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/il-fs-group-stocks-plunge-up-to-14-pc-on-rating-cut-118091000795_1.html)
- Contagion: NBFC stocks kept falling in the following weeks (the financial-services index -7% vs the benchmark -3.7% on one day; Dewan Housing about -34.5% and Central Bank about -33.2% over 14-21 Sep 2018, per press) with analysts pointing to liquidity and commercial-paper exposure [S]. — [BS, 9 Oct 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/here-s-how-the-il-fs-crisis-spread-to-nbfc-stocks-in-indian-markets-118100901109_1.html), [BS, 24 Sep 2018](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/nbfcs-extend-fall-bajaj-finance-indiabulls-housing-down-over-5-118092400232_1.html). No formal event study of the episode was found.
- NSE "Credit Rating" corporate-announcement feed (my probe, a few requests, cookie session): JSON records with announcement date, symbol, ISIN, description "Credit Rating", an attachment PDF on nsearchives.nseindia.com and an XBRL flag; the free-text field is generic ("X has informed the Exchange about Credit Rating"), so direction is not in the JSON [P-probe]. Equity-universe counts returned: 2010-11 about 90/yr (180 over two years), 2014 = 156, 2018 = 896, 2023 = 1,573 (monthly 72-183), Oct 2024 = 118, Oct 2025 = 146 [P-probe]. Filings appear back to at least 2010. Counts include reaffirmations. Whether the endpoint caps rows per call is unverified. Example attachments: [ONGC 30 Oct 2026 filing](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ONGC_30102026192117_Creditrating30102026.pdf), [Rolex Rings 31 Oct 2026 upgrade intimation](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ROLEXRINGS_31102026101121_Intimation_for_upgradation_of_Credit_Rating.pdf). Endpoint pattern: [NSE corporate announcements API with subject Credit Rating](https://www.nseindia.com/api/corporate-announcements?index=equities&from_date=20-10-2026&to_date=31-10-2026&subject=Credit%20Rating); human page: [NSE corporate filings](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-announcements).
- Agency sites: CARE robots.txt is allow-all [P]; CRISIL robots.txt blocks about 27 named bots including GPTBot, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, bingbot and Scrapy and disallows press-release, newsletter and DAM paths for all agents [P via fetch summary]; India Ratings has WordPress-style disallows only [P via fetch summary]; ICRA robots path returned nothing usable (ASP.NET app, no robots file found) [P]. Terms of use were not read. — [CARE](https://www.careratings.com/robots.txt), [CRISIL](https://www.crisilratings.com/robots.txt), [India Ratings](https://www.indiaratings.co.in/robots.txt), [ICRA](https://www.icra.in/robots.txt)

### Inferences
- The two Indian studies are not reconcilable from abstracts: different years (1991-2015 vs 2009-2019), different universes, and one reports a sign split that the other averages away. Both are consistent with "downgrade matters more than upgrade", which also matches the US pattern, but the Indian downgrade effect size is unquantified.
- For a long-only delivery book the realistic use is an AVOID flag (skip or exit a name after a downgrade or negative outlook), which adds no round-trip cost on names not bought. The IL&FS day-1 gap (up to -14% at the first open after a Sunday action) shows an after-the-fact exit cannot dodge the first move; only a pre-event screen (rating close to cut, heavy commercial-paper reliance) could, and that needs data I did not find.
- The NSE feed is the cheapest route to a dated list for the 2010-2026 window the reader's price file covers, but the direction field has to be parsed from about 1,500 PDFs a year [C: 2023 count]; XBRL is flagged on the records, which would be the lighter parse. Cost to build is analyst time, not market cost.

### Gaps
- Dichev and Piotroski (2001), J. Finance 56(1):173-203 (Moody's, 1970-1997) was cited as the US benchmark but no primary page was opened and no URL captured. Magnitude: I saw "one-year abnormal returns of 10-14% in size after downgrades" only through a secondary summary; not verified in the primary abstract [S]. Jorion-Liu-Shi (2005) and Holthausen-Leftwich (1986) were not retrieved.
- Indian studies seen only as listings and not opened: a 1996-2015 study (CRISIL reaction larger than ICRA/CARE; negative reaction after both upgrades and downgrades; smaller after the global financial crisis but not significantly), "Has the GFC changed the market response to credit ratings?" (page redirected 307), a BSE-100 2010-2018 initial/reaffirmed-rating study, and "Price and Volume Effects Around Credit Rating Change Announcements: Evidence from India" (Global Business Review, Dec 2024; the metatoc page timed out, abstract only seen earlier, no bp numbers captured). The IIMB WP 134 and the Paradigm 2001 paper were not read.
- Direction-filtered event counts per year (downgrades only) are unknown; the NSE counts above mix upgrades, downgrades, reaffirmations and withdrawals.
- SEBI's credit-rating-agency disclosure/master circular (the rules that require agencies to publish rating histories and default/transition statistics) was not retrieved (sebi.gov.in was unreachable from my fetcher), so I cannot state what history agencies must expose.
- No India-specific evidence on bank-loan-rating downgrades or on rating actions on unlisted-debt-only issuers was found.
- Whether NSE's terms of use allow bulk collection of this API was not checked; see section 5 for the access caveat.

## 3. OFS, disinvestment and PSU effects

### Takeaway
Quantified Indian OFS evidence is thin (abstracts only: 39 NSE OFS cases, 66 OFS, 18 PSU FPOs), but all point the same way: price weakness before and after announcement, strongest in PSUs, with the 2026 wave (23 OFS deals) showing 5-7% announcement-day falls on 8-12% floor discounts. Nobody has published a post-OFS holding-period return table, so this is an untested, testable event family with dated, machine-readable events.

### Cited Findings
- Maheshwari and Chatnani (2025), Review of Behavioral Finance 17(3):442-461: 39 NSE OFS cases, Mar 2012-Jul 2023; prices fell significantly BEFORE the announcement in both cash and derivatives for PSU companies (read as floor-price leakage) and in cash only for non-PSU companies. No numbers (paywalled) [P abstract]. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/rbfpps/rbf-07-2024-0208.html)
- Basu, Jain and Singhal (2016), Research Bulletin 42(1):148-169: 66 OFSs; improper floor-price setting destroys wealth; price-movement patterns that intraday and retail traders could use to avoid immediate losses; an arbitrage opportunity for institutions at the expense of existing shareholders. Period and numbers not stated (paywalled, INR 400) [P abstract]. — [ICMAI](https://icmai-rnj.in/index.php/rb/article/view/99112)
- Sehgal, Kumar and Gupta (2014), Global Journal of Emerging Market Economies 6(2):181-202: 18 PSUs with FPOs, 2002-early 2013; IPO abnormal returns positive; FPO-1 positive before the public notice then negative to the offer-price date; FPO-2 negative after the notice and continuing past the offering; discounts shrink over stages. No numbers [P abstract]. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/emeeco/v6y2014i2p181-202.html)
- Tripathi and Singh (2025), Journal of Social and Economic Development 27(2):408-421: ten utility-sector firms, NIFTY-50 and NIFTY-CPSE benchmarks; no CAR, windows or test statistics in the abstract; conclusions are about efficiency, not returns [P abstract]. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/jsecdv/v27y2025i2d10.1007_s40847-024-00350-8.html)
- Ghosh and Aithal (2022): not an event study; post-disinvestment ROA/ROCE/ROE fall (negative for fundamentals) [P]. — [MPRA 112056](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/112056/)
- 2026 OFS wave: askfuzz counts 20 companies across 23 OFS deals raising Rs 62,730 crore in 2026, of which government companies Rs 58,425.12 crore [S]. — [askfuzz](https://askfuzz.ai/discover/news/equity-markets/ofs-fundraising-hits-record-62-730-crore-in-2026)
- Announcement-day moves (press, early-trade figures): Central Bank of India fell about 6% as its OFS opened on 22 May 2026 [S] — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/central-bank-shares-tank-6-why-psu-stock-fell-as-ofs-kicks-off-532790-2026-05-22); IRFC fell nearly 5% on a 7.5% floor discount [S] — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/stocks/irfc-share-price-falls-nearly-5-as-government-launches-ofs-at-7-5-discount); NLC India floor Rs 303, a 12% discount, up to 3% (2% base plus 1% greenshoe) [S] — [Mergers India](https://mnacritique.mergersindia.com/news/govt-to-sell-up-to-3-stake-in-nlc-india-via-ofs-at-%e2%82%b9303-per-share/).
- Post-OFS snapshots (not a systematic study): Bank of Maharashtra 1-month -3%, 3-month +9% around its OFS [S] — [Samco](https://www.samco.in/knowledge-center/articles/bank-of-maharashtra-ofs-government-begins-stake-sale-as-share-price-holds-steady/); of 11 PSU stocks with government stake above 75%, 10 had negative one-month returns as of 2 June 2026, Central Bank about -18% [S] — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/irfc-mazagon-dock-mrpl-ntpc-green-sjvn-shares-why-are-psu-stocks-with-high-govt-stakes-falling-534418-2026-06-02); a headline claims PSU stocks gained up to 83% after OFS (NLC ~83%, NHPC ~79%) but the article returned HTTP 402 and was not read, and such lists are survivor-prone [S headline only] — [Zeebiz](https://www.zeebiz.com/market-news/news-nlc-india-ofs-opens-as-government-completes-6-stake-sales-in-2026-why-psu-stocks-have-gained-up-to-83-after-ofs-396944).

### Inferences
- Maheshwari-Chatnani implies the OFS information is partly priced before it is public (PSUs especially), so an announcement-day or post-announcement entry is buying after the leak. That makes pre-announcement weakness a candidate AVOID overlay on PSU names, not a long entry.
- The long-run OFS rate in the Maheshwari sample is about 3.4 a year [C: 39 events over Mar 2012-Jul 2023 = 11.4 years], so the 2026 count of 23 is about 7 times that [C: 23 / 3.4]. A rule fitted on 2012-2023 behaviour will be tested mostly on a regime that has never repeated.
- Cost is not the obstacle: an OFS holding period of a month or more pays 35-40 bp once, against announcement-day drops of 5-7% and a 3-month +9% anecdote. The obstacle is power: with N = 39 to 60 events the detectable mean effect at t = 2.865 is 0.46 to 0.37 times the per-event standard deviation [C: 2.865 / sqrt(39) and sqrt(60)], and OFS events cluster in time (same day, same policy shock), which makes the effective N smaller.
- Mixed snapshots (Bank of Maharashtra -3% then +9%; 10 of 11 high-stake PSUs down at 1 month; headlines claiming +79 to +83%) are consistent with "short-run overhang, then mean-reversion", but also with survivor-selected press; none is evidence.

### Gaps
- The retail-quota rule (the 5% retail discount, retail-day T+1 bidding return, listing/next-day return) was NOT verified. I could not fetch the SEBI OFS circular (sebi.gov.in DNS failure from my fetcher). From a search snippet only [S]: SEBI approved the framework in Jan 2012, promoters and holders of 10% or more are eligible, and at least 10% is reserved for retail. A "Coal India retail 11% discount" figure appeared only on low-quality mirror pages (accred.deac.org, hdndhy.hungyen.gov.vn, lbsim.ac.in paths that look like search-spam on compromised sites) and is treated as unverified; do not use.
- Coal India (floor about 10% below previous close; fell 4-6%) and Hindustan Copper (floor 10.5% below Rs 574.15; fell nearly 7%) numbers came from a search summary without a recoverable URL; Central Bank discount of about 8.6-9.4% likewise [S].
- FY26 receipts conflict (Rs 16,886 crore in one report vs a DIPAM table figure of Rs 8,768 crore); unresolved.
- No systematic post-OFS 1/3/6-month return table exists in what I found; no study of the T+1 retail day; no paper on non-government (promoter) OFS returns beyond the 39/66-case abstracts.
- Yearly OFS counts for 2012-2026 from the NSE feed were not collected (see section 5, the loop was blocked). The Basu et al. (66 OFSs) period is unstated.
- No formal Budget-day PSU event study or study of government disinvestment-target announcements was found; the Business Standard items are headlines.
- The SEBI minimum public shareholding (25%) deadline logic that makes government-stake above 75% names OFS candidates was not sourced here; the shareholding-pattern data source (NSE/BSE quarterly pattern) was not tested.

## 4. News and media-sentiment signals with documented returns

### Takeaway
The only documented net-of-cost news results are US (Ke-Kelly-Xiu 2019: gross 19 bp/day on the long leg, 94.6% daily turnover) and they would not survive an Indian 35-40 bp round trip; Tetlock (2007) is an index-level, temporary effect. Indian news-sentiment papers report predictive fit or short-lived effects with no real out-of-sample trading test, so I found no Indian news-sentiment signal with a documented, tradable return.

### Cited Findings
- Ke, Kelly and Xiu (2019), NBER w26186, "Predicting Returns with Text Data": Dow Jones Newswires 1989-Jul 2017, out-of-sample Feb 2004-Jul 2017 [P]. Day+1 equal-weighted long-short 33 bp/day, Sharpe 4.29, daily turnover 94.6%; value-weighted 10 bp/day, Sharpe 1.33 [P]. Equal-weighted long leg 19 bp/day (Sharpe 2.12), short leg 14 bp/day (Sharpe 1.21), alpha almost all of the return [P]. Day-0 (same day) long-short 93 bp/day (Sharpe 10.78) and day -1 45 bp/day (Sharpe 5.88): most of the move precedes or coincides with the news [P]. RavenPack-based equal-weighted Sharpe 3.2 vs Loughran-McDonald 1.7 [P]. Stale news is fully reflected in about 2 days and fresh news in about 4 days; small and volatile stocks respond about 4 times more [P]. After a 10 bp/day cost with a turnover-controlled portfolio: gamma 0.5 gives gross 20.43 bp/day, net 11.23 bp/day, net Sharpe 2.30; gamma 0.1 gives gross 5.18, net 3.58, net Sharpe 1.17 [P]. — [NBER w26186](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26186)
- Tetlock (2007), Journal of Finance, WSJ "Abreast of the Market" 1984-1999: a one-standard-deviation rise in pessimism lowers the next-day Dow Jones return by 8.1 bp (t 3.94; unconditional mean 5.4 bp), followed by a reversal over lags 2-5 of 6.8 bp; the five-lag sum (-1.3 bp) is not significantly different from zero. Negative words -4.4 bp and weak words -6.0 bp with reversals of 9.5 and 10.7 bp [P]. — [Tetlock 2007 PDF](https://www.columbia.edu/~pt2238/papers/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf)
- Tetlock, Saar-Tsechansky and Macskassy (2008), Journal of Finance, S&P 500 firm-specific WSJ/DJNS stories 1980-2004: the fraction of negative words forecasts low earnings and a next-day price reaction, with trading profits offset by transaction costs [S, research-page summary; I did not extract the trading-strategy numbers]. — [Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/more-words-quantifying-language-measure-firms-fundamentals)
- Chari et al. (2023), Journal of Risk and Financial Management 16(8):376: aggregate news sentiment has only a short-lived effect on Indian returns (business and politics coverage); GARCH/regression, no tradable out-of-sample test [S; MDPI returned 403, abstract via search only]. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/16/8/376)
- Sankaranarayanan (2025), Investment Management and Financial Innovations: Times of India business articles scored by FinBERT with a PCA index; reverse causality (returns drive sentiment), small sentiment effect [S]. — [Business Perspectives author page](https://businessperspectives.org/journals/author/k-g-sankaranarayanan)
- SENTInews (Nath et al.): LSTM return forecast with "high accuracy"; the out-of-sample design is not visible in what I saw [S]. — [CatalyzeX author page](https://catalyzex.com/author/Gourab%20Nath)
- Kataria and Agarwal, IIIT-Delhi 2018 thesis on Indian financial news and returns: restricted access [S]. — [IIIT-D repository](https://repository.iiitd.edu.in/jspui/handle/123456789/790)

- News data availability: NSE corporate announcements are company-issued filings, not media; media archives are restricted by their robots files (Moneycontrol and ET entries in section 1) [P]. No licence-free, dated, machine-readable Indian news-headline archive was verified.

### Inferences
- KKX arithmetic at Indian costs [C]: daily turnover 94.6% x a 35-40 bp round trip = 33-38 bp/day, against a long-leg gross of 19 bp/day (the long-short gross of 33 bp/day needs shorting, which delivery trading cannot do). Net is negative by roughly 14 to 19 bp/day on the long leg alone. This assumes turnover is the fraction of the book replaced each day and that one replacement pays one round trip; if KKX defines turnover one-way the cost halves but the long leg is still at best break-even.
- The reported edge sits where a late reader cannot reach it: day 0 (93 bp) and day -1 (45 bp) dominate day +1 (33 bp), and decay completes in 2-4 days, so a days-to-months holding horizon captures none of it.
- The net-of-cost KKX result (11.23 bp/day net at gamma 0.5) exists only because the authors damp turnover; that is a portfolio-construction fix needing a full-text news feed the reader does not have.
- Indian papers disagree on direction of causality (Sankaranarayanan finds returns drive sentiment); a news signal built on them would be circular. The project's own NifSent50 labels are circular for the same reason, per my earlier read of the covered notes.

### Gaps
- TSM (2008) trading-strategy return and cost numbers were not extracted (my scratch PDF extract turned out to be a different paper, Feldman-Govindaraj-Livnat-Segal on MD&A tone, which I did not use). The qualitative claim is from a summary page only.
- Saxena et al. (ASIANCON 2021) and other Indian ML papers seen in listings gave no out-of-sample metrics; no URL recovered for Saxena.
- No Indian study with an honest walk-forward, cost-aware trading test on news sentiment was found. I did not look at RavenPack or Bloomberg India coverage quality, or at GDELT/Common Crawl as free sources (not verified).
- LLM-based Indian headline classifiers (2024-2026) were not systematically searched beyond one query.
