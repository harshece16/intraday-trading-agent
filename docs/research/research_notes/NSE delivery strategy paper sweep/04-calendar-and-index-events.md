# Calendar, seasonal and index-event effects for NSE delivery trading

Conventions used below. "Cost bar": the delivery round trip is about 35-40 bp for a stock (33.05 bp at Rs 33,000, 39.05 bp with doubled slippage, about 41 bp at Rs 3 lakh, 56-59 bp at Rs 5,500-6,667 per name; [project cost table](file:///Users/harshgupta/Projects/webapp/docs/research/research_notes/NSE%20delivery%20strategy%20paper%20sweep/00-already-covered.md), section 6) and about 5-7 bp plus spread for an NSE-listed ETF (the project's own turn-of-month sweep used about 10 bp). "bp" figures marked "(my arithmetic)" are conversions I made from a source's per-day or per-year number. Candidate ids C1-C30 are used again in the last section. Evidence stops: the newest Indian peer-reviewed calendar test I found ends in Feb 2023 (Satish and Bheemanagouda); I found no 2024-2026 academic test of any calendar effect on NSE data.

## Turn-of-month, dash-for-cash and payday effects: does the evidence exist for India, and how large is it?

### Takeaway
Turn-of-month (TOM) is the best-documented calendar effect (about 60 bp per 4-day window in the US, present in 31 of 35 countries), but India was not in the McConnell-Xu sample and the Indian papers I found give significance without effect sizes, so the only India magnitude is the project's own ~41 bp gross per window. It clears the cost bar for an ETF on paper and does not clear it for individual stocks; the project has already marked it "Leave".

### Cited Findings
- McConnell and Xu (FAJ 64(2), 2008): window = last trading day of the month (day -1) plus first three trading days of the next month (days +1 to +3); US 1926-2005 plus 34 non-US countries to 31 Jan 2006; effect present in 31 of 35 countries; mechanism described as a puzzle (volume and flows not elevated); costs not reported — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v64y2008i2p49-64.html), [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month), [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)
- US size: from 1987 the mean daily value-weighted return in the window is 0.14-0.15% against about -0.01% to 0.00% over the other 16 days (equal-weighted 0.24% vs 0.04%); across 1926-2005 the VW window-minus-other-days difference is 0.15%/day with t = 7.81 and about 7.2% a year across about 48 in-market days (Sharpe about 1.04) — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=819), [Purdue-hosted copy of the FAJ article (scanned; read by OCR)](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43), [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/turn-of-the-month-in-equity-indexes)
- India was excluded from the McConnell-Xu sample: the authors dropped India "for infrequent trading in years before 1994" (they also dropped Bangladesh, Jordan and Nigeria) — [Purdue-hosted copy, endnote 6](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43). So "31 of 35" says nothing about India.
- Countries with no meaningful TOM effect in that sample: Argentina, Colombia, Italy, Malaysia; Taiwan 0.12% vs 0.00% with t = 1.59 for the difference; Greece 0.34% vs 0.00% — [Purdue-hosted copy](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
- Quarter-end vs other months: the VW TOM premium is 0.14 pp at quarter-ends and 0.16 pp at non-quarter-ends, and the authors conclude the evidence does not attribute TOM to fund managers dressing quarter-end returns — [Purdue-hosted copy](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
- Etula, Rinne, Suominen and Vaittinen, "Dash for Cash" (RFS 33(1):75-111, 2020): market returns are negative before settlement day T-3 (T-4 in the slides; the two secondary sources disagree on the label) and strongly positive in the 3 business days after; the pattern is stronger in later years and where mutual-fund industries are larger; attributed to institutions raising cash for month-end outflows — [Aalto](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/dash-for-cash-monthly-market-impact-of-institutional-liquidity-ne/), [SUFE newsletter](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/352443)
- India TOM, Satish and Bheemanagouda (Review of Finance and Banking 15(2):101-118, 2023): Nifty 50, Sensex, Nifty SmallCap 50 and BSE SmallCap, April 2007 to Feb 2023; TOM effect significant and more pronounced in the small-cap indices; abstract gives no bp or t-statistics — [RFB](https://rfb.ase.ro/Vol15-2023/dec2a.asp); companion note [RFB](https://rfb.ase.ro/Vol15-2023/dec2r.asp)
- Older Indian TOM studies: Mangala and Sharma, Nifty 1994-2005 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/padigm/v11y2007i2p16-22.html)); a Sensex 1998-2007 study ([i-Scholar](https://i-scholar.in/index.php/ijf/article/view/202255)); also a TOM paper on the Indian currency market ([Emerald](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJMF-05-2014-0068/full/html)). Abstracts only; no effect sizes retrieved.
- Project's own India number: "~41 bp gross vs ~10 bp round trip" and "~41 bp gross/window vs ~10 bp ETF round trip" — profitability-sweep-2026-10-01.md:154 and data/profitability-sweep/R4-low-turnover-families.md:19, as quoted in [00-already-covered.md](file:///Users/harshgupta/Projects/webapp/docs/research/research_notes/NSE%20delivery%20strategy%20paper%20sweep/00-already-covered.md) section 6c
- Payday anomaly, Ma and Pratt (SSRN 3257064): about 2.57% a year, Sharpe about 0.6, reported as weakening — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/payday-anomaly)

## Day-of-week, holiday, Diwali, Budget, month-of-year, Halloween and lunar effects in India

### Takeaway
Indian evidence for these effects is a patchwork of abstract-only, short-sample papers that contradict each other (Sasidharan finds none; others find September, November, January); the practitioner Diwali and Budget tallies have no significance tests. The one robust-looking cross-country effect (Halloween) was reported absent in 11 NSE indices over 1994-2014. None is a strong candidate; post-holiday and Budget-week are worth a cheap event study only because each is one position a year or a few.

### Cited Findings
- Sasidharan 2009 (MPRA 24185): once outliers are handled there is no seasonality in Indian market returns — [MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/24185/1/MPRA_paper_24185.pdf)
- Sen 2014 (Journal of Commerce and Accounting Research 3(2)), Sensex 2004-2012: September and November effects found — [Publishing India](https://www.publishingindia.com/storage/PDFBrochures/2312.pdf)
- Pandey 2002 (IIMA): Sensex monthly seasonality, January effect, 1991-2002 — [IIMA](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/there-seasonality-sensex-monthly-returns)
- SAJGBR 2014 (11 NSE indices, 1994-2014): no Halloween effect, a strong September effect (sign not retrieved) — [Emerald](https://www.emeraldinsight.com/doi/abs/10.1108/SAJGBR-07-2014-0050)
- Halloween elsewhere: Bouman and Jacobsen (AER 2002) found it in 36 of 37 markets and it weakened after publication in Europe; Jacobsen and Zhang (108 markets, 1693-2011) find November-April beats May-October in 81 of 108 indices, significantly in 35 and significantly negative in 2, about 4.5 percentage points a year on average; a CFA blog (2012) says it is "stronger than ever" — [AEA](https://benny.aeaweb.org/research/charts/halloween-spirits), [Jacobsen-Zhang PDF](https://www.bnains.org/backtest/periode/The_Halloween_Indicator_everywhere_and_all_the_time_-_Ben_Jacobsen_%26_Cherry_Y._Zhang.pdf), [CXO Advisory](https://cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/halloween-effect-pervasiveness), [CFA blog](https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/investor/2012/10/30/the-halloween-indicator-a-stock-market-anomaly-that-is-stronger-than-ever). India's country row was not found; a 28-country 2000-2011 paper does not mention India ([MPRA 41539](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/41539/)).
- Day-of-week: Indian studies are mixed and several report the weekday pattern disappearing after the move to rolling settlement; no bp tables retrieved — [MPRA 46805](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/46805/1/MPRA_paper_46805.pdf), [JCAR](https://publishingindia.com/archive/jcar/the-day-of-the-week-effect-in-the-indian-stock-market-revisited)
- Holidays, Lu and Patel (Economics Bulletin 36(2):851-856, 2016), BSE indices: strong post-holiday effect, pre-holiday effect "completely missing"; no numbers in the abstract — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ebl/ecbull/eb-16-00286.html)
- Lunar: papers exist but are weak and mostly non-Indian — [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/filter.php?aid=104783), [LSE paper](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/yuan/papers/lunar.pdf), [IJICBM](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ids/ijicbm/v2y2009i3p281-294.html)
- Diwali (practitioner, not peer reviewed): median Nifty return in the 90 days after Diwali 4.71% and in 365 days 9.32%, no unconditional comparison or test — [Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2020/11/does-diwali-bring-cheer-to-nifty-a-look-at-the-odds/); Muhurat session up in 15 of 20 years — [Finnovate](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/20-years-muhurat-trading-historical-returns)
- Union Budget (practitioner): 2004-2023, index up on the day before the Budget in 13 of 20 years, on Budget day in 8 of 20 and the day after in 13 of 20 — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/trading/how-do-major-indices-react-to-union-budget/article-65567/); one-week pre-Budget average return -0.52% and the following week +1.36% (Navia reproduces the figures for 2010-2022; other pieces describe a 15-year window, so the period is not consistent across sources; I did not find the IANS original) — [Navia](https://navia.co.in/blog/investors-guide-navigating-volatility-in-the-wake-of-budget-2024/); Elara: -1.6% over the month before the Budget since 1999 — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/markets/markets-give-negative-returns-a-month-ahead-of-budget-115022500131_1.html); an arXiv note covers only four Budgets and is not usable — [arXiv 2502.15787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15787)
- Same-calendar-month stock seasonality (Heston-Sadka 2008; Keloharju, Linnainmaa and Nyberg): stocks that did well in a calendar month tend to do well again in that month in later years — [NBER w20815](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20815.pdf); effect sizes and any India test were not retrieved.

## F&O expiry-week and expiry-day effects, including the 2024-2025 SEBI changes

### Takeaway
The only expiry effect with a published size is the US option-expiration-week premium (about 52-70 bp per expiry week, 12 weeks a year); Indian studies show expiry days change volume and volatility but I found no tested return premium, and the 2024-2025 SEBI and NSE rule changes post-date every Indian study I could read. The documented Indian edge is in option writing, which the project has ruled out of mandate.

### Cited Findings
- Stivers and Sun (option-expiration week), via Quantpedia: S&P 100 stocks, backtest 1988-2010; hold large caps in the option-expiration week (12 weeks a year), cash otherwise; indicative 9.3% a year including cash, of which 6.3% from the 12 weeks; Sharpe 0.61; volatility 8.7%; max drawdown -15.14%; return spreads of 67.9 and 70.7 bp in the two subperiods; transaction costs "Not Reported"; no decay discussion — [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/option-expiration-week-effect)
- Singh and Shaik (2020), Nifty futures, 74 expiration dates from April 2013 to June 2019 (37 before and 37 after Bank Nifty weekly options started): volume on expiry days differs significantly from comparison days; after Bank Nifty weekly options began, returns were higher, volatility and volume lower; no coefficients in the abstract — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eco/journ1/2020-03-3.html)
- Chhimwal, Pandey and Pandey (Review of Derivatives Research 28(3), 2025), 1-minute data around NSE moving Bank Nifty weekly and monthly expiry from Thursday to Wednesday: connectedness between Nifty and Bank Nifty fell, volume and volatility rose for both indices, authors read this as an expiry-day anomaly and say it contradicts the exchange's aim of cutting expiry-day volatility; no magnitudes in the abstract — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/revdev/v28y2025i3d10.1007_s11147-025-09221-8.html)
- Monthly expiry Thursdays since 1999 (260 expiries): the one-day Nifty 50 TRI change from the day before expiry to expiry day looks random — [FreeFinCal](https://freefincal.com/best-day-for-sip-last-thursday-of-month-nifty-fo-expiry-date/)
- SIP timing: an arXiv paper finds a monthly SIP executed on expiry day beats one on the first trading day by 0.5-2.5% a year over 1-3 year horizons; not peer reviewed — [arXiv 2507.04859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04859v1)
- Regime changes: SEBI limited each exchange to one weekly-expiry index derivative from 20 Nov 2024 — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/nse-ends-weekly-expiry-for-key-index-derivatives); NSE moved weekly and monthly expiries from Thursday to a Tuesday effective 1 Sep 2025 (some secondary sources say Monday; I did not resolve which) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/nse-bids-adieu-to-thursday-expiry-as-dates-swap-come-into-effect-explained-125082800635_1.html)
- SEBI's interim order against Jane Street alleging index manipulation on expiry days (July 2025) — [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/sebi-update-interim-order-against-jane-street-group-for-alleged-index-manipulation/); SEBI study: F&O traders' net losses widened in FY25 — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)

### Inferences
- **C17 Option-expiration-week (OE-week) long, Nifty ETF or large caps.** Stivers-Sun via Quantpedia / S&P 100 1988-2010 / about 6.3% a year over 12 weeks = about 52 bp per OE week (my arithmetic) or 68-71 bp per week spread / t not retrieved / 12 a year / 5 sessions / gross, costs not reported / robust in both halves and ex-2008 per the source; no decay data / India: none found for the return premium (Singh-Shaik tests volume and returns on the expiry day only) / daily closes plus the NSE expiry calendar suffice, but the calendar has changed (Thursday to Tuesday, one weekly contract) so the OE week must be re-defined / ETF clears on paper (52-70 bp vs 5-10 bp); stocks barely (52-70 vs 35-40 at 12 turns a year) / index-level / overlap: not in 00-already-covered.md; the Indian OE week is the last week of the month, so it overlaps the lead-in to the TOM window (C1) and the pre-turn trough in C3; it should be tested together with them, not as a separate family.

## Month-end and quarter-end flows: NAV window dressing and portfolio pumping

### Takeaway
US mutual funds inflate quarter-end NAVs by about 50 bp a year (large caps) to over 200 bp a year (small caps) through last-day trading, reversed the next day; I found no Indian test beyond one thin abstract, and McConnell-Xu show the turn-of-month premium is the same size at quarter-ends and other month-ends, so window dressing does not explain it.

### Cited Findings
- Carhart, Kaniel, Musto and Reed (JF 57(2):661-693, 2002): equity fund returns net of the S&P 500 are abnormally high on the last day of the quarter (especially the fourth) and abnormally low the next day; magnitudes around 50 bp a year for large-cap funds to well over 200 bp for small-cap funds; little or no effect at month-ends that are not quarter-ends; the authors tie it to "marking up" or "portfolio pumping" through aggressive late trading and regulators treat the practice as a concern — [Carhart et al. PDF](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/carhart_leaning_for_tape_JF.pdf)
- McConnell-Xu: the VW TOM premium at quarter-ends (0.14 pp) is almost identical to that at non-quarter-ends (0.16 pp) — [Purdue-hosted copy](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/ciberwp/43)
- India: one conference abstract on mutual-fund quarter-end behaviour (Chowdary and Banerjee); no results retrieved — [IIM Calcutta](https://studentlive.iimcal.ac.in/sites/all/files/pdfs/abstract_bobbur_abhilash_chowdary.pdf)

### Inferences
- **C20 Quarter-end window-dressing / portfolio-pumping reversal (long into the last day, out the next).** Carhart et al. 2002 / US funds, late 1990s era / 50-200+ bp a year of NAV inflation, and the stock-level price pressure is not given in my retrieval / t not retrieved / 4 a year / 1 night / gross / not replicated outside the US in my sources; US enforcement attention suggests decay (my inference) / India: none found / daily closes plus calendar / even a 200 bp-a-year effect is about 50 bp per quarter-end and is spread across many funds' holdings, so a stock-level trade at 35-40 bp round trip does not clear, and an ETF carries the fund-level effect only in tracking terms / index and stock / overlap: not in the covered list; adjacent to the overnight ETF (closed).

### Gaps
- No Indian quantification of quarter-end NAV inflation or the associated stock-level return; AMFI daily NAVs and portfolio disclosures would be needed.
- Carhart et al. sample years and daily stock-return numbers: not extracted (PDF text only partly read).

## Index events: Nifty inclusion and exclusion, MSCI and FTSE flows, F&O entry, and rebalance pre-positioning

### Takeaway
Indian evidence on Nifty inclusion and exclusion is contradictory (price pressure that reverses; a permanent information effect; no tradeable effect), but one study finds stocks dropped from the Nifty drift up 4-7% over 60-240 days after a 10-day dip, a daily-scale long-only trade that would clear the delivery cost bar if it survives a proper event study. The index-event premium has largely vanished in the US (Greenwood-Sammon 2025).

### Cited Findings
